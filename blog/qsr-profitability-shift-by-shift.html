QSR Profitability Is Built Shift by Shift

In quick-service restaurants, the margin is won or lost in hundreds of small decisions every day. Here is where to look.

Riishi KalawatiaNovember 2026 · 5 min read

These lessons come from more than eight years with Jubilant FoodWorks as Assistant Unit Business Head across channels. Quick-service restaurants prove that profitability is not decided in the boardroom. It is decided shift by shift, order by order, by the team in the kitchen and on the counter.

1. Food cost hides in preparation, not purchasing

Purchase prices matter, but the bigger leaks are usually inside the kitchen: over-portioning, preparing too far ahead of demand, and wastage at the end of the day. Clear portion standards, demand-based preparation and daily wastage tracking protect margin more than most negotiations with suppliers.

2. Labour should follow the demand curve

Restaurant demand has sharp peaks around meal times and weekends, with quiet periods in between. Rosters that match the demand curve, hour by hour, reduce cost and improve service at the same time. Under-staffing the peak loses sales; over-staffing the quiet hours loses margin.

3. Delivery and dine-in are different businesses

As delivery has grown across India, the Middle East and beyond, many restaurants run delivery and dine-in from the same kitchen. They share a kitchen but not a customer experience. Delivery needs packaging that protects food quality, accurate order assembly and tight dispatch times; dine-in needs ambience and table service. Measure them separately.

4. Speed of service is a sales driver

In quick service, speed is part of the product. Track order-to-delivery and order-to-table times, find the bottleneck station during the peak, and fix it. Faster service increases capacity without adding a single square foot.

5. Teams stay where they grow

High team turnover is one of the largest hidden costs in food service. Clear training paths, from crew member to shift lead to manager, give people a reason to stay and make every shift stronger.

The takeaway

Strong QSR brands look effortless because their operations are disciplined. Control food cost in the kitchen, match labour to demand, treat delivery as its own channel, and grow your people. Profit follows.
